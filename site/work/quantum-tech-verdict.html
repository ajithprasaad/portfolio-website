An engagement to turn a deep-tech platform with no confirmed market into a defensible go/no-go call.

00 / context

The client was a University of Maryland I-Corps venture with a quantum optimization and control platform. Strong technology, no confirmed market. Three options looked plausible internally, and none had been tested with a customer. I owned the assessment: the interviews, the analysis, and the final recommendation.

01Problem

The platform could serve three markets: computer science, defense and logistics, and energy grid optimization. Each had a sponsor inside the team. None had been tested with a customer.

The wrong call would cost years of a small team's runway. The engagement was framed around one question: which market, and how do we know?

02Situation analysis

One pattern did the work. Industry expectations run about a decade ahead of the hardware. Fault-tolerant quantum is not available, so value has to come from current NISQ-class devices. That gap is why most quantum commercialization stalls.

03Approach
01

The recommendation was locked to a rule before the interviews

A candidate earned a recommendation only if roughly 30% of interviews independently corroborated it. Below that bar, any market looks good in a deck.

02

Map the market through the buyer

Interviews traced users, decision makers, and the people who influence a purchase. As a finding crystallized, I extended the network to the engineers and managers who would use the platform.

03

Score every candidate on the same four criteria

Market size, technology fit, customer readiness, and time to value. Applied identically to each candidate, then weighed against the interview evidence.

04Evaluation

The same criteria were applied to all three candidates. Two were ruled out for entry. One earned the recommendation.

Computer science, defense, logistics

No-go for entry. Incumbents are entrenched and procurement runs long, and the hardware gap makes that wait worse.

Energy grid optimization

Hold. The need is real, but infrastructure lock-in and multi-year sales cycles outweigh the near-term value.

Pharma, drug discovery

Go. Molecular optimization is NISQ-compatible, sits inside a $160B market, and was the only candidate that cleared the corroboration bar.

3-Phase
GTM plan adopted by the team

Drug discovery became the primary focus, and the team adopted the three-phase plan. The control angle became a defined next test instead of a claim: interview quantum hardware control engineers before treating them as a second segment.

06Verdict & GTM

Go on drug discovery as the entry market.

The tradeoff is explicit. The quantum slice of the drug-discovery market is still small, and I accepted that in exchange for time to value. When fault-tolerant hardware is a decade out, a market you can serve with current devices beats a bigger one locked to that wait.

Re-evaluation trigger: if no startup pilot is signed by the end of phase one, the call gets revisited.

Phase 1

Startup pilots

Prove the near-term hybrid with pharma startups that will experiment for an edge. This is also the cheapest way to get real revenue and CAC data.

Phase 2

Enterprise pharma

Convert pilot evidence into partnerships, the pattern Boehringer Ingelheim and Roche already run with quantum suppliers.

Phase 3

Test the control segment

Interview quantum hardware teams before building for them. The research proves the technology; only customers can prove the market.

Pharma drug discovery

Quantum hardware / control

Control engineers and VPs of engineering at quantum computing companies. Technically proven in published work, set for customer interviews in phase three.

07Reflections

What worked: the interviews turned a plausible bet into a call with a rule behind it. The readiness gap made the decision legible, and the three-phase plan gave the verdict an execution path.

What I would test next: validate revenue and CAC economics through the startup pilots, and interview the control segment before pricing it as its own market. Both are the cheapest ways to confirm the call, and both are within reach of a single pilot round.
